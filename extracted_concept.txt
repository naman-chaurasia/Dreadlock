AT A GLANCE01. Project Snapshot

DREADLOCK is built around a single dramatic promise: players are not fighting a monster together; they are taking turns being the person who runs through danger and the person who controls it. The facility forces that reversal every 120 seconds, making adaptation the heart of the competition.

ITEM

DEFINITION

Genre

Asymmetric 1v1 competitive horror / strategy / arcade

Platform / engine

PC prototype in Unreal Engine; exact engine version follows the existing project

Session

Two human players; online host/join is the target

Camera

Guest: third-person by default. Warden: fixed surveillance/control-room view.

Match format

Four 120-second rounds; roles alternate after each round. Three arenas rotate, with one arena repeated.

Primary player fantasy

Outthink a human opponent while a supernatural facility changes the rules around you.

Tone

Oppressive, mysterious, supernatural; no gore required

Target build

Playable vertical slice suitable for a 24-hour game hackathon

Design pillars

Asymmetric fairness: both roles have meaningful decisions, clear counterplay and equivalent match time.

Readable horror: threats feel surprising, but signals give players a fair chance to react.

The building is the weapon: doors, lights, lifts, barriers and traps are gameplay systems, not decoration.

Role reversal changes perspective: each player must understand both sides to win consistently.

Small scope, strong identity: compact arenas and reusable systems instead of an open world.

Core design rule

Every sabotage action must create a readable, avoidable problem for the Guest and a meaningful strategic choice for the Warden. Random punishment is not good multiplayer horror.

NARRATIVE FOUNDATION02. Story Bible

Logline

Two strangers awaken inside a supernatural facility built by a vanished civilization. One must cross its lethal chambers; the other must control them from a sealed dark room. Every 120 seconds, the facility swaps their roles. The only way to earn freedom is to master both sides of the same deadly system.

The premise

The facility is called the Crucible by the people trapped inside it. Its creators are known only as the Architects: an ancient civilization that treated consciousness as a force that could be measured, divided and rewritten. They built structures that respond to thought, memory and intention. Their greatest experiment was not a weapon. It was a trial designed to discover which minds could adapt when control and vulnerability changed without warning.

The two captives wake in separate spaces. The Guest occupies the physical facility: a vast complex of stone, metal and impossible geometry. The Warden awakens in a sealed control chamber, surrounded by camera feeds, levers, power routing controls and a voice that speaks through the walls. Both receive the same message: “Earn your exit.” Neither is told that only one clearance will be granted.

Backstory

At the height of their civilization, the Architects built the Crucible to test whether a mind could survive absolute changes in agency. One subject was given movement but no control over the environment. The other received control over the environment but no physical freedom. The facility linked their decisions through a supernatural system called the Covenant Engine. Every two minutes, the Engine reversed the assignment. The Architects believed a mind that could survive both power and helplessness could be trusted with the civilization’s final technology.

The experiment outlived its creators. The Architects vanished, but the Crucible continues to run its trials. It no longer remembers the purpose of the experiment. It remembers only the procedure, the punishment and the promise of an exit.

The central mystery

The facility claims that the winning captive will be released. Evidence found in later arenas suggests that previous winners were not freed; they were absorbed into the system and became part of its control network. The final decision is whether to accept the promised exit, destroy the Covenant Engine, or risk remaining inside long enough to release both captives. For the prototype, this mystery is communicated through props, short transmissions and the ending screen—not expensive cinematic sequences.

Narrative direction

Keep the story legible through short environmental clues. The competitive round must remain the main event; lore should reward attention without interrupting play.

Recommended match format

RULE

PROPOSED SPECIFICATION

Players

Exactly 2 human players in a match.

Round length

Exactly 120 seconds of role time. The server owns the timer.

Rounds per match

4 rounds / 8 minutes maximum, not counting menus and transitions.

Role assignment

Roles alternate at every round boundary. Each player gets two Guest rounds and two Warden rounds.

Arena sequence

Arena 1, Arena 2, Arena 3, then replay one arena for the final round.

Early objective result

Completing the exit objective locks the Guest round win. The 120-second window continues for optional bonus actions; roles swap at the scheduled boundary.

Final result

Most round wins takes match. Total score breaks a tie; if still tied, run a 60-second sudden-death round.

Round flow

Ready check → role assignment → 3-second countdown → 120-second live round → lock round result if the exit objective is completed, while optional bonus play continues → round-end horn → switch roles → load/reset next arena → final results after Round 4.

Fairness rule: the server controls role switching and timer state. Both clients must observe the same round index, role assignment and remaining time.

Warden / Saboteur actions

ABILITY

EFFECT

BALANCE GUARDRAIL

Lockdown

Temporarily locks a selected door or barrier.

Short duration; visible warning; cannot seal every route.

Blackout

Turns off local lights and disrupts one camera feed.

Emergency lights remain; Guest can navigate; cooldown is longer.

Trap trigger

Activates a floor hazard or pressure mechanism.

Wind-up cue and clear escape lane; no direct damage kill in MVP.

Power reroute

Enables one hazard or disables one helpful system.

One active reroute at a time; energy cost prevents spam.

Resource model

Warden energy: starts each round at 100 units. Suggested action costs: Lockdown 25, Blackout 35, Trap 30, Power Reroute 40. Tune after testing.

Energy recovery: regain 10 units every 20 seconds, capped at 100. No ability may bypass cooldown or energy validation.

Cooldowns: each ability has its own visible cooldown. Do not rely on energy cost alone to balance a dominant ability.

No instant-kill sabotage in the prototype. The Warden wins through denial and time pressure; this is clearer to balance in a short hackathon build.

Environmental interaction minimum

At minimum, ship three functional mechanisms in every arena or prove at least three unique interactive elements across the game. For a stronger judge demonstration, reuse a small set of replicated mechanism classes with different level placements.

Level layout rules

Use a compact loop with a safe spawn zone, central objective, one alternate route and a final exit point.

Never place the Guest so close to a hazard that a Warden can guarantee a hit before the first warning cue.

Place cameras so the Warden must choose between complete route coverage and monitoring objective interactions.

Keep the control room visually distinct, but avoid building a second large 3D map. A fixed camera console with clickable feeds is enough for the MVP.

Make navigation differences readable through landmarks, lighting and unique silhouettes; do not rely on color alone.

MVP map production

Block out all three arenas using the same scale and interaction framework. Each arena should be completable in a 120-second role window after the team learns its route. If time slips, keep three small functional stages rather than one polished stage and two empty maps.

Asset strategy

Reuse doors, switches, trap volumes, light actors, moving platforms and UI widgets. Variants should come from placement, timing and visuals—not bespoke code per arena.

Role-switch rule

At each round boundary, the server swaps role assignments in one atomic transition: Player A switches from Guest to Warden while Player B switches from Warden to Guest. The server increments RoundIndex, resets round timer and role-specific resources, loads or resets the next arena, then begins the next countdown. A player must never be Guest on one client and Warden on the other.

Failure and disconnect handling

CASE

EXPECTED BEHAVIOUR

Player leaves mid-round

Pause briefly and show a disconnect banner. End round as a forfeit after a short grace period; do not hang forever.

Host disconnects

For MVP, terminate session safely and return the remaining player to the menu. Host migration is out of scope.

RPC spam / wrong role

Reject request on server; do not change state. Add modest rate limiting if time allows.

Round timer reaches zero twice

Round resolution must be idempotent. Only one transition and one score award may occur.

Arena fails to load

Return to a known safe map and show a recoverable error; never leave players in a black screen indefinitely.

Role UI fails to refresh

Drive HUD from replicated role/phase state, not a local toggle. Rebuild role UI on OnRep/state change.

Critical test: force a role swap while both clients are connected. Verify roles, controls, HUD, camera access, energy and replicated mechanism states all agree.

Match winner

Four rounds; each player is Guest twice and Warden twice.

Primary result: most round wins. Secondary result: highest total points across all four rounds.

If tied after four rounds, run a 60-second sudden-death round in a selected arena. Swap roles only after that round resolves.

The fiction states the winner earns the only exit clearance; the loser is reclaimed by the facility. This preserves a clear competitive stakes model.

Fairness and anti-frustration rules

Each trap has an activation cue, delay and counterplay route. Never make a perfect-information, unavoidable trap the dominant strategy.

Cap points from repeated use of one mechanism so a Warden cannot farm score without making strategic choices.

Keep role time equal, reset role-specific resource state predictably and show why an action failed.

Test both player identities in both roles; do not test only Player 1 as Guest.

PRESENTATION-READY15. Pitch, First Demo and Glossary

One-sentence pitch

DREADLOCK

A two-player asymmetric supernatural horror game set inside a facility built by a vanished civilization. One player races through shifting chambers while the other controls traps from a sealed surveillance room. Every 120 seconds, the facility swaps their roles, forcing both players to master escape and sabotage to earn freedom.

30-second judge pitch

“DREADLOCK turns the building itself into the opponent. Two players are trapped in a supernatural facility built by a vanished civilization. One explores and completes objectives; the other controls the environment through cameras, doors, lights and traps. Every two minutes, they switch roles, so neither player can win by mastering only one side. Three compact arenas, limited sabotage energy and a server-controlled scoring system create a fast, replayable mind game.”

First 90 seconds of a live demo

Show both players connected from separate computers. Identify Guest and Warden clearly.

As Guest, approach the first objective and start interacting.

As Warden, use one visible sabotage mechanism. Show the Guest receive a warning and choose counterplay.

Show the timer and role transfer. Demonstrate that the same player now controls the opposite role.

Finish by showing the arena selection and scoring/results interface. Do not claim untested features.
